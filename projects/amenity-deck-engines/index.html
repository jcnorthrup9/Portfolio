Amenity Deck Engines

Two Grasshopper and Python engines that lay out an amenity deck — pools, BBQ zones, waterways and hardscape — on the podium of a high-rise tower, structured into three privacy zones off the building's main egress points. The second engine replaced the first, and that replacement is the point.

The first is a six-phase pipeline. It reads the deck boundary and its fixed obstacles — mechanical plant, egress stairs, entrances — off the Rhino document, buffers them, and runs a straight skeleton through what is left, seeding pools along the centrelines and expanding them to a target deck-to-water ratio. A weighted Voronoi power diagram resolves the overlaps into obstacle-clipped cells; a last phase turns each into a buildable basin, grid-aligned, inset by a coping width, and typed automatically into a lap pool or a composite basin with a Baja shelf.

It worked, and the shapes were wrong. The power diagram answers the question it was asked — which pool owns which square foot — but its cells read as arbitrary. What it was genuinely good at was telling me where the zones wanted to be.

So I drew the three zones by hand over the engine's own placements, and wrote a second, much smaller engine that builds the pools straight from those curves: an inward offset for the upper pool, grid-aligned basins along the site axis for the middle, a capped soaking pool for the lower. No skeleton, no power diagram, no catalogue. The algorithm became the instrument, not the author.

*The two photoreal perspectives are Rhino renders finished with AI enhancement. Every drawing and diagram here is output from the definitions.
